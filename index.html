Key insights for a first-time bidder

Everything below is computed from this July 7, 2026 sale — not generic advice.

Most listings never make it to sale
89 of 279 sold (32%) · 167 postponed (60%) · 23 stayed

Roughly 2 in 3 get postponed or stayed, often the morning of the sale. Don't build a plan around one address — track several, because most will vanish before the gavel.

The “minimum bid” is a floor, not the price
Median winning bid ran 463% over the opening minimum · only 3 of 89 (3%) sold at/near opening

The minimum (the “upset price”) is just the plaintiff’s costs and taxes — unrelated to market value. Expect to pay several times it; budget accordingly.

Most sales go back to the bank
63 of 89 (71%) sold to the plaintiff/lender · only 26 to an outside buyer

“Sold to Plaintiff” means the lender repossessed — no investor outbid the debt. The 26 true third-party wins are the real acquisition opportunities.

Third-party wins are pricier and hard-fought
3rd-party: median $126,800, 733% over min, ~14 bids vs plaintiff: $81,900, 314%, ~10 bids

When outside investors want a property, expect a bidding war — up to 62 bids on a single lot this sale.

You’re bidding nearly blind on the debt
68 of 89 (76%) sold for LESS than the debt owed

The debt/upset figure tells you little about value. Do your own comps, and a title/lien search — sheriff sales convey as-is and some liens can survive.

Come cash-ready
$5,035 deposit required to bid on any property · $1,000 bid increments · all-cash, no financing

You must have the deposit pre-placed to bid, and the balance is due fast. There’s no financing or inspection contingency.

Where the money went (this sale)
Busiest ZIPs: 19144 (7 sold, avg $255,528), 19143 (7 sold, avg $80,939), 19151 (6 sold, avg $92,020), 19134 (6 sold, avg $56,887)

By price: 40 sold under $100k, 30 in $100k–$200k, 19 above $200k. Lower-priced ZIPs move on volume; higher-value ZIPs draw the big bids.

Note: Educational summary of public auction results — not legal, financial, or investment advice. Sheriff-sale properties sell as-is with no inspection; verify occupancy, condition, taxes, and title/liens independently before bidding.
